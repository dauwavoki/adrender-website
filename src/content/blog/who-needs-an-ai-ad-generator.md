Everyone with a product wants ads. That's not a useful observation. The interesting question is who needs *many* ads, fast — and who actually profits from making more of them. An Artificial Intelligence (AI) ad generator is wasted on someone who needs one hero image a quarter. It's built for people whose business gets better every time the number of live tests goes up.

Here's who that actually is.

**Freelance ad designers**

If you make ads for other people's brands, your ceiling used to be hours. Clients now want more variations per engagement than a manual workflow can absorb, because paid social rewards variety. The designers who can turn around ten on-brand static and video versions in the time it used to take to make two can raise rates, take more gigs, or both — without the quality drop people assume AI requires. [How freelance ad designers scale clients without working more hours](/blog/freelance-ad-designer-scale-clients).

**UGC creators**

User Generated Content (UGC) creators hit a hard physical limit: you can only film so much. Brands still want more hooks and angles tested before they pay for a full shoot, and they want volume in the gaps between shoot days. AI-generated video is a scaling layer for that testing and pitching work — not a replacement for the filmed, authentic content that's still the premium offering. [How UGC creators use AI video to take on more brand deals](/blog/ai-ugc-ads-creator-scaling).

**Agencies and media buyers**

As platforms automate targeting and bidding, creative volume is the lever left. Winning accounts run more active variations than losing ones. Small agency teams cannot produce 20+ new static and video ads a month per client by hand without burning out or handing the margin to freelancers. [Why agencies can't stop testing ads — and how to produce the volume without hiring](/blog/creative-volume-agency-testing).

**Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) and Shopify sellers**

If you sell your own products, the reason you need more ads is simpler: Cost Per Mille (CPM) keeps climbing, and a tired creative is how that shows up in your account. Testing more variations — new hooks, new angles, new formats — is how you stay ahead of fatigue instead of noticing it after you've already overpaid. [Beat creative fatigue by refreshing before Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) climbs](/solutions/creative-fatigue).

**Affiliate marketers and promoters**

Affiliate and promoter accounts live on constant creative tests. Offers rotate, angles die fast, and you're often running someone else's brand without a designer on retainer. There is no dedicated post for this group because the job is the same job as everyone else's on this list, just faster: more static and video variations, on-brand enough to not look stolen, shipped this week. Volume is the product.

**Already working with a freelancer or agency?**

You don't have to pick a side. The gap most brands actually feel is the one between "I need ten more ideas tested this week" and "the person I hired can turn ten more ideas around by Friday." AI fills that gap — cheaper tests before you commission the expensive work, clearer briefs, coverage when your freelancer is booked. [How AdRender fits alongside a freelancer or agency](/blog/hiring-a-freelancer-or-agency-adrender-fits-in).

**The common thread**

None of these people need a complicated tool that requires prompting and design skill. They need speed and volume, because the algorithm rewards whoever tests the most. A prompt box hands the hardest part of the job back to the person who came here to get more ads out the door. A blank canvas does the same thing with extra steps.
